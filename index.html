Analyzing Cobb 500 Broiler Growth under Malaysian Tropical Heat Stress

In commercial Malaysian poultry farming (Semenyih, Johor, Kedah), broilers are highly susceptible to heat-induced stress due to high ambient temperatures (28°C to 34°C) and relative humidity exceeding 80%. In your animal husbandry studies, optimizing the Feed Conversion Ratio (FCR) is key to profitability.

By applying differential calculus, we model broiler weight over time using a sigmoidal growth function f(x). The first derivative f'(x) represents the Instantaneous Daily Growth Rate (grams/day). Finding the inflection point where f''(x) = 0 identifies peak biological feed efficiency, helping Malaysian managers schedule the exact optimal harvesting day to avoid wasting feed on thermal maintenance.

Simulation Parameters

Asymptotic Max Weight (L): 3,400g
Growth Velocity (k): 0.125
Hatch Weight (W0): ~42g

Growth Function: f(x)

The cumulative body weight of the broiler at age x (days) modeled by the Logistic Growth Curve:

f(x) = L / (1 + a * e^(-k * x))
• L: Maximum asymptotic weight (g)
• a: Biological scaling constant (a ≈ 80)
• k: Growth rate coefficient
• x: Age of the broiler (days)

Daily Weight Gain: f'(x) (Derivative)

The rate of change of cumulative weight with respect to time, giving daily weight gain (g/day):

f'(x) = k * f(x) * (1 - f(x) / L)
Husbandry Secret: As weight f(x) approaches L (mature limit), growth rate f'(x) declines to 0. The peak rate occurs exactly at f(x) = L/2 (the Inflection Point).

Thermal Deceleration (f'(x) Drop)

Under heat stress (Open-House model), the first derivative f'(x) peaks much sooner but at a significantly lower magnitude. Due to hyperventilation (panting), energy is diverted to respiration instead of cellular protein synthesis.

Inflection Point (f''(x) = 0)

This point indicates the peak daily growth velocity. Past this point, growth decelerates (negative acceleration). For broiler farmers, FCR starts deteriorating quickly after this point, marking the start of the selling window.

Economic Cutoff Decision

Once f'(x) falls below 50g/day (in late stages), keeping chickens alive is unprofitable as cumulative feed cost exceeds cumulative weight revenue. Derivatives solve the optimal broiler marketing problem instantly.
